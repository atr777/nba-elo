# The Engagement Playbook: what to restack and comment on, daily

**2026-07-26.** The research in
`docs/research/2026-07-26-substack-growth-and-virality.md` found that below 10k
subscribers, in-niche engagement and recommendations outrank everything else, and
that creators who do not restack or comment stall regardless of content quality.
The Notes feed is no longer follower-gated, so this is how a small publication gets
seen. This file is the daily routine. **It is Aaron-only work**: it has to come
from a human account, and it is judgement, not automation.

Budget: **8 to 10 minutes a day.** Phone is fine. Substack app, Notes tab.

## 2. The daily routine

**Two restacks, three comments, roughly 9 minutes.** Consistency matters far more
than volume; the algorithm is reading audience overlap, not effort.

**Step 1, restack twice (3 min).** Open the Notes feed. Find two notes from Tier A
or B and **restack with a note**, not a bare restack. A bare restack is nearly
invisible; adding a line puts our voice into the feed as its own item. One should
be someone smaller than us (generosity gets returned), one larger (reach).

**Step 2, comment three times (5 min).** Leave one substantive comment on three
different publications. Substantive means it survives without our link.

**Step 3, log it (1 min).** Note who you engaged with, so the same five names do
not absorb every day. A rotating list of 15 beats a loyal 3.

Weekly, on top of the daily loop: **two recommendation asks** (section 4).

## 3. What our comments should actually say

We have one asset nobody else in a comment thread has: **a number, and a public log
that predates the conversation.** That is the whole angle. Bring a datapoint, and
never bring a pitch.

**The four moves that work for us:**

1. **The specific number.** They make a claim; we add what our system says, with
   the scale attached so it is legible. "For scale, that is X in our ratings where
   1500 is average."
2. **The honest disagreement.** Our model differs from their take. Say so plainly,
   give the number, and concede the uncertainty. "Our model reads that differently,
   it has them at N. One of us is wrong and it will be settled in public."
3. **The confession.** Reference a miss of ours that is relevant. This is the most
   disarming thing we can do and almost nobody does it. "We had that game at 88%
   and lost it."
4. **The methodology note**, only when genuinely useful. Explain a trap we hit, not
   a feature we shipped. People remember the person who saved them a mistake.

**What not to do**, because it reads as marketing and gets muted: leading with our
accuracy figure, dropping links unprompted, "great post!", correcting someone's
grammar or their team loyalty, arguing about a player's value without a number,
posting the same phrasing twice in a week, and any em dash.

**Tone check:** we are the numbers desk, not the takes desk. Dry, specific, a
little amused. The model is a character we speak about in third person; the humans
say "we."

**Worked examples** (adapt, never paste verbatim, and verify any roster claim at
the moment of writing per the roster-accuracy rule):

> On a "Sixers are contenders now" post: "Our roster model has them plus 45 points
> of talent this summer, top three in the league. It still only moves them to 12th,
> because they finished last season below the line. Both things are true at once
> and that is the interesting part."

> On a modelling post: "We spent a week fitting draft-position priors for rookies
> and the whole thing came out a null, Brier moved 0.00001. Posting it because the
> negative results are the ones nobody publishes."

> On a confident prediction from someone else: "Calibration is the part that gets
> skipped. When ours says 80 to 90 percent it wins 79.6, and we only know that
> because every pick is logged before tip-off."

## 4. The recommendation asks (the highest-leverage item in the whole project)

Recommendations plus the app drive about half of all new Substack subscriptions,
and we currently receive none. Two asks per week, sent as a Substack DM or a reply
to a note, after you have engaged with them a few times so it is not cold.

**The ask, in our voice:**

> I run Second Bounce, an NBA prediction model with a fully public track record:
> every pick logged before tip-off, graded after, nothing recomputed. 70.6% across
> 657 games last season, and we published a correction downward when we caught our
> own tracker flattering us. I have been reading [specific thing they wrote] and
> already recommend you. Happy to add a recommendation either way, no obligation.

**Start with Tier A, and lead with Binomial Basketball.** Same premise, different
method, so the swap is genuinely interesting to both audiences rather than
competitive.

**Also switch on our own recommendations** for every Tier A publication we
genuinely rate. Recommending others costs nothing, is visible to them, and is the
opening move that makes the ask land.

## 5. The visual format: THE RECEIPT (chosen 2026-07-26)

Our house image for Notes is a **thermal-receipt slip**: monospace on torn paper,
1080x1350 portrait. Chosen from three candidates because BRAND.md already names the
receipt as our one ownable signature and we had only ever rendered it as a one-line
stamp. Nothing else in NBA media is shaped like it, and it is inherently
screenshot-shaped, which is the format the feed rewards.

    python scripts/generate_receipt_note.py season       # games, record, accuracy
    python scripts/generate_receipt_note.py calibration  # stated vs actual

Every figure is read from `prediction_tracking_honest.csv` at render time, never
typed in, so a receipt cannot drift from what the audit would reproduce.

**Do NOT put an image on every note (Aaron, 2026-07-26).** A feed where every post
carries a graphic reads as an automated broadcast, and the research is clear that
casual, observational notes travel too. **Target roughly one in three.** The split:

| Gets a receipt | Stays text-only |
|---|---|
| A claim that wants proof ("70.6% across 657 games") | Observations and opinions |
| Record, calibration, graded results | One-liners in the model's voice |
| Anything a sceptic would want to verify | Confessions and asides |
| Season and weekly summaries | Replies and conversational notes |

Landscape images get a warning from the drip. Our existing charts are all landscape
because they were built for the newsletter; re-render for the feed rather than
reusing them.

## 6. What this is not

This is not automation, and it should not become automation. The Notes drip
(`scripts/drip_notes.py`) handles our own broadcast on a schedule. Engagement is
the part that has to be a person reading, because the algorithm is measuring
whether real audiences overlap and readers can smell a bot in a comment thread
instantly. Ten honest minutes beats a hundred generated replies, and the downside
of getting caught automating comments is the credibility we are actually selling.
