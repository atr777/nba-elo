# Free Agency Day One, Through the Model's Eyes

The NBA rearranged itself overnight. Giannis to Miami. Jaylen Brown to Philadelphia for Paul George and a pile of picks. Kawhi back to Toronto. Ja Morant to Portland. LeBron telling the Lakers he's playing his next season somewhere else.

Everyone has takes. The model has ratings. Here's day one graded by numbers, not vibes.

![Giannis Antetokounmpo, the headline move: model rating 2,280](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/df546ea5-8f62-4531-94e7-baf764306c6e_1456x780.jpeg)

A quick primer for new readers: every player in our system carries a rating where 1500 is a league-average player, built from box-score impact across the season and adjusted for what raw stats over- and under-count. These are the numbers our prediction engine actually uses, the same engine that went a verified 70.6% last season.

![Free agency day one movers ranked by model rating](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/7f735dd0-4017-4787-bdad-d73871945cd7_1456x1120.jpeg)

## The headline: 2,280 points of Giannis just moved

Giannis is the highest-rated player in our system, and it isn't close to the rest of this list. Milwaukee finished last season rated 1403, which is bottom-five territory, and just lost the only reason that number wasn't worse. Miami finished at 1505, almost exactly league average. Dropping the model's best player onto an average roster is how a play-in team becomes a problem. The full effect depends on who else Miami keeps, but no other single move on this board compares.

## Boston sold high, by exactly 47 points

The model rates Jaylen Brown at 1746 and Paul George at 1699. Strictly on current player value, Boston got worse by 47 rating points, which is real but modest. Then add two first-round picks and two seconds, and remember Boston finished at 1673, still one of the strongest teams in the league. This reads like a contender converting present value into optionality without leaving the contention window. Philadelphia, rated 1487 and starving for shot creation, paid draft capital to consolidate talent. Both sides can be right, but the model thinks Boston made the safer bet.

## The Kessler tax

Here's the nerdy one. Walker Kessler's raw rating is 1699, which looks like a fringe star. Our adjusted rating, the one the engine actually uses, is 1529. Why the haircut? Box scores flatter rim protectors: blocks, rebounds, and low turnovers inflate their numbers relative to how much they actually swing wins. It's the same adjustment we carry on Gobert and most of the league's rim-protecting bigs, and last season the adjusted numbers predicted games better than the raw ones.

The Lakers reportedly just paid Kessler $130 million over four years. The market priced the raw number. The model prices the adjusted one. One of us is wrong, and we'll find out in public.

## The rest of the board

Kawhi at 1858 is a massive add for a 1520-rated Toronto team if his body cooperates, which has been the asterisk on Kawhi for a decade. LaMelo at 1703 lands on a Minnesota team that finished 1605, and he carries our shot-creator premium, so the model likes the fit more than the discourse seems to. Ja Morant at 1647 gives Portland a genuine engine but doesn't move a 1492 team into the West's upper tier by himself.

And LeBron, rated 1947 at age 41, is the biggest unresolved number on the board. Wherever he lands moves that team's forecast more than any signing announced today except Giannis.

## What the model can't see yet

Honesty section. These ratings are end-of-last-season snapshots. They don't yet know about fit, minutes, or age curves, and the team numbers won't fully absorb these moves until our preseason ratings build in September, when all thirty teams get the complete roster-math treatment, one team per day.
