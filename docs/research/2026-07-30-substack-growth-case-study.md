# What to take from "20,585 New Subscribers in 90 Days" (2026-07-30)

Source: Kaguura Gichuru, *The Write Path*,
`kaguura.substack.com/p/90-days-20585-new-subscribers-heres`. Aaron sent it. This is
the digest, what we are changing, and what we are deliberately not.

## Read the result honestly before copying the method

The headline is 20,585 subscribers in 90 days. The mechanism, by the author's own
account, was one essay that caught fire after **a restack from Michael Burry**, going
to ~250,000 impressions and taking the publication from 2,000 to 13,000 in a month.

The method did not cause the Burry restack. So the tactics below are best read as
**the conditions that make a lucky break convertible**, not as an engine with a
predictable rate. The author says as much: "luck happens out of nowhere, but being
ready for it is entirely up to you." Any plan of ours that assumes 5,000 subscribers
a month because someone else got it is a plan built on someone else's lottery ticket.

One more calibration. The author writes in "philosophy, psychology, business," which
on Substack is an ocean. We write about NBA prediction accuracy, which is a pond. Our
ceiling for the same effort is genuinely lower, and that is not pessimism, it is the
denominator.

## The finding that contradicts what we have been doing

**Growth came from commenting on other people's posts, not from broadcasting.** Three
layers: weekly essays, Notes extracted from them, and high-signal comments on larger
publications. The author is explicit that the comment sections were the visibility
engine, and that link-dropping and "great post!" replies are worthless.

We have done **zero** of that. We built a Notes drip that broadcasts into a feed where
we have essentially no followers, and got exactly what that predicts: 21 likes, **0
restacks**, 0 post reactions, 1 subscriber. Restacks are the only mechanism that
reaches a stranger, and nothing we have published has earned one.

I also steered us to X as the seeding channel, which this case study does not use at
all. That was my recommendation and it has produced nothing measurable so far.

## The neighbourhood question, checked rather than assumed

"Comment on larger publications" only works if larger adjacent publications exist. I
checked (2026-07-30, via each publication's public archive endpoint):

| Publication | Latest post | Reactions | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neil Paine | 2026-07-29 | 25 | 3 |
| Basketball Analytics Lab | 2024-10-16 | 9 | 2 |
| NBA Insights | 2026-06-13 | 5 | 0 |
| Gary Marcus | 2026-07-29 | **228** | **79** |

**The NBA-analytics corner of Substack is thin.** One publication is dormant since
2024, one is small and quiet. The standout is **Neil Paine**: active daily, and his
current post is *MLB Elo Power Ratings*, which is our exact method in another sport.
He is the single most relevant person on the platform for us.

The busy room is one step sideways: **model evaluation and AI-claims skepticism**,
where Gary Marcus alone is pulling 79 comments a post. Our leakage essay is native to
that audience. It is a story about a model overclaiming and being caught by its own
author, which is that room's entire preoccupation.

So the target is not "NBA fans on Substack." It is **forecasting and model-honesty
readers, reached via the one essay of ours that already speaks their language.**

## What we are changing

1. **Commenting becomes a real channel, and it is Aaron's to run.** Comments have to
   be genuine and in his voice; I cannot ghost-write them without producing exactly
   the empty flattery the article says fails. My job is targets and angles.
2. **Notes get an Awareness tier.** The author's three types are Awareness (a human
   observation that stops a scroll), Education (explain a useful idea), Conviction (a
   contrarian line that filters). Our queue is almost entirely Education and
   Conviction: numbers and claims. We have close to nothing that a person who does not
   care about ELO would stop for. Three Awareness notes added this session.
3. **The welcome email gets written.** `welcome_email_body` is **empty** on the
   publication right now, so the handful of people who do subscribe get a subject line
   and nothing else. At one subscriber, wasting a first impression is expensive.
   `hero_text` is empty too.
4. **Titles carry a number.** "How We Caught Our Own Model Cheating" is decent but
   vague on value. The author's rule is a specific number plus a clear promise. Our
   next post is the preseason table, so: a ranked 30-team forecast with a stated date
   it can be graded on.

## What we are NOT taking

- **Broad niche framing.** The author widened to avoid burnout. We cannot widen without
  dissolving the product, which is a specific public track record. The useful version
  of this idea is not a wider topic, it is a wider *frame*: forecasting honesty,
  demonstrated through the NBA.
- **Restack swaps and recommendation trading.** The article lists both under what did
  not work. I nearly proposed recommendation trading two sessions ago; noting it here
  so it stays proposed-and-rejected rather than getting re-suggested.
- **Paywall advice.** Moot. Substack paid is Stripe-only and Stripe does not support
  Guatemala. See [[substack-paid-tier-blocked]].
- **The cadence target as stated.** One essay plus 3-5 Notes weekly is reasonable, but
  our Notes cadence (2/day) already exceeds it while our essay cadence does not meet
  it. The binding constraint here is essays, not Notes.

## The strategically important one, which is a decision for Aaron

The author does not monetize subscriptions at all. Essays are free and act as "a
billboard for your brain"; revenue comes from **backend services**, ghostwriting,
consulting, advisory, speaking. The viral essay produced $2,000+ in a month through
consulting inquiries, more than a paywall would have.

**That model fits our situation better than it fits the author's.** Aaron already runs
a consulting practice (GEO / AI-visibility, iCatch Group). Second Bounce is a live
demonstration of exactly the skills that practice sells: building a measurable system,
auditing it honestly, publishing the receipts. The correction essay is a better
credential for a data-and-measurement consultant than any case study deck.

And it routes around the blocker completely. **We do not need Substack payments if the
revenue is Aaron's services rather than subscriptions.** The paid tier being
impossible in Guatemala stops being critical path.

This is not mine to decide, and it changes what "success" means for the project, so it
is written here as a question rather than actioned. It sits alongside Aaron's stated
position (2026-07-30) that having the project built is itself a success.
